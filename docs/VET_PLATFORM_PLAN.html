Pet10x Veterinary Platform

Software a veterinary practice runs its day on — customers, appointments, reminders, storefront — that any clinic can sign up for in two minutes, and that gets quietly better for every customer who happens to be a Pet10x owner.

00 · What this is

Revision 1 designed Pet10x as the custodian of a medico-legal record, which brought a regulatory programme with it. Your reframe removes that. What is left is a much better first product, and a much cheaper one.

What changed, and why it helps

Pet10x holds regulated clinical recordsNo clinical data written here; the service owner keeps their own data, owners fill and share their own recordsremoves the retention, custody and controlled-drug obligations from the near term. This is the single biggest simplification.

The product in one paragraph

A veterinary practice signs up on their own, imports their client list from a spreadsheet, and within an afternoon is booking appointments on a multi-provider calendar, checking animals in, and sending vaccination reminders that a receptionist can work as a call list. None of that requires a single customer to have a Pet10x account. Then, for the customers who do, four things get better without anyone configuring them: the owner books online, the reminder arrives as a free push notification instead of a postcard, the owner can hand over their pet's history in one tap, and the clinic can push a vaccination record back into the owner's app where their building's compliance file picks it up.

The strategy is unchanged even though the scope shrank. Pet10x wins on the side of the relationship no practice-management vendor can reach — the owner's phone and the owner's building. Release one earns the clinic's daily habit; releases three onward make that habit pay off in a way a standalone scheduling tool never can.

01 · The line: operations, not charts

"No clinical data" needs one careful distinction, because a vet visit obviously produces some record. The line I am proposing is between a service record and a clinical record, and it is the boundary the whole first release sits inside.

In scope service records

What the business did, for whom, when, and what it cost. The same shape of record a groomer or a boarding kennel keeps.

  • Appointment happened, who saw the animal, how long
  • Services performed, from the clinic's own price list
  • Vaccination given: product, date, next-due date
  • A free-text visit note the clinic writes for itself
  • Attachments the clinic uploads — a photo, a scanned form
  • Next visit due, follow-up flag
  • What was charged and whether it was paid

Out of scope for now clinical records

The structured medico-legal chart. Deferred to the EMR track, where it belongs with the review it needs.

  • Structured SOAP notes with signing authority
  • Prescriptions with prescriber authority and refill law
  • Controlled-drug custody register
  • Lab orders, results and reference ranges
  • Problem lists and formal diagnosis coding
  • Amendment trails and immutability guarantees

The practical test: if a groomer could keep the same field without anyone raising an eyebrow, it is a service record. "Rabies vaccine, 14 March, due again 14 March 2029" passes. "Differential diagnosis: suspected early renal insufficiency" does not.

Why this line is safe to hold. A free-text visit note is the clinic's own business record about their own customer — exactly what service_bookings already permits today for every other business type. Nothing in release one is a category Pet10x has not already handled. The moment the product asks a vet to sign something, or tracks a scheduled drug, or computes a dose, it has crossed the line — and that is the EMR track in §15, with its own review.

What the bridges are, precisely

  • The link (clinic_patients.pet_id, nullable) says only "this customer record is about that Pet10x pet". It grants no access on its own. This one nullable column is what lets a practice serve customers with and without Pet10x accounts through exactly the same screens.
  • The share is the owner opening a scoped, expiring, revocable window onto their own records. §09.
  • The publication is the clinic handing a record back — "we gave this vaccine, here is the date" — which lands in the owner's app carrying the clinic's name. §09.

This is a pattern the codebase already runs. Accommodation requests work the same way: the resident supplies documents, the manager verifies them, and a database trigger stops either side rewriting the other's half — a rule even administrators cannot bypass. The team is not learning a new idea, only applying a familiar one to a second pair of parties.

03 · Self-serve signup and capability tiers

Any practice can sign up unaided and be working the same afternoon. But "signed up" and "may read a stranger's pet records" have to be two different states, or the sharing feature becomes a hole anyone can walk through with a free email address.

The answer is not gatekeeping the signup. It is tiers: everything a clinic does with their own data is instant and unrestricted; the capabilities that touch someone else's data unlock as the practice proves it is a real business.

Tier 0
Registered

Instant, self-serve, no review. Email verified, that is all.

Full use of the console: customers, patients, appointments, scheduling, visit notes, reminders to their own contact list, invoices, storefront draft, staff and locations. Everything in releases one and two.

Cannot: appear in Pet10x search, take bookings from Pet10x owners, receive shared records, publish records, or use emergency access.

Tier 1
Listed

Light automated check — business name, address, phone reachable, a real website or a confirmed location.

Adds: appears in owner-facing search and the marketplace, accepts online bookings from Pet10x owners, shows a public storefront.

Tier 2
Verified clinic

Licence or registration number recorded and checked. A person reviews it once.

Adds the capabilities that touch another person's records: receiving owner record shares, publishing records back into an owner's app, appearing in emergency surfaces, and emergency access. §11.

Tiers are also a growth mechanic. A practice that has been using the calendar for a fortnight has an obvious reason to finish verification — their customers start arriving through the app.

The signup route must be rebuilt, not extended

The existing business signup endpoint creates a confirmed, role-elevated account for any email address with no verification at all, and enforces only an eight-character password. It is an open security finding in the diligence review, and it is the exact door this module would be inviting thousands of businesses through. Rebuilding it onto the existing one-time-code flow is roughly two days of work and is a prerequisite for release one — not a nice-to-have.

Import is a release-one feature, not a later one. With no partner and no onboarding call, a practice that must retype four hundred customers will simply close the tab. 05 · Scheduling

The calendar is the product. It is what a practice opens at 8am and closes at 6pm, and if it is not better than the paper book or the spreadsheet they are using, nothing else in this plan matters.

Appointment lifecycle

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> requested : owner books online, pending confirmation
  [*] --> booked : staff books directly
  requested --> booked : clinic confirms
  requested --> declined : clinic cannot take it
  booked --> arrived : checked in at reception
  arrived --> in_progress : taken through
  in_progress --> ready : treatment done, awaiting checkout
  ready --> completed : checked out, visit recorded
  booked --> no_show : did not arrive
  booked --> cancelled : cancelled by either side
  booked --> rescheduled : moved to a new slot
  cancelled --> [*]
  no_show --> [*]
  completed --> [*]

Enforced by a database function with an explicit transition table, not by which buttons happen to render. The diligence review found five state machines in this codebase enforced only in the interface, and one product bug that fell straight out of that — appointments start on the right side of that line.

What the calendar has to do

Structure

  • Day, three-day and week views, per location
  • Column per provider, or per room, switchable
  • Appointment types carry their own duration — a dental books ninety minutes, a nail trim fifteen, without anyone remembering
  • Resources: consult rooms, theatre, grooming bay
  • Provider working patterns, holidays, blocked time
  • Recurring appointments for course treatments

Reality

  • Drag to move, resize to extend
  • Double-booking allowed with a warning, because practices genuinely do it
  • Walk-in queue that is not on the grid but is on the screen
  • Emergency slots held back and released at a set hour
  • Overrun visible — running forty minutes late shows on the board
  • No-show recorded, counted, and visible on the customer record

Two things only Pet10x can do here

Online booking that fills the book

A Pet10x owner books from their phone against the clinic's real availability and appointment types. Requests can require confirmation or auto-confirm by type — a vaccination auto-confirms, a surgical consult asks. Every booking taken this way is a phone call the practice did not have to answer.

Waitlist that actually fills cancellations

A cancellation at 9:40 for an 11:00 slot is money gone. Waitlisted owners who are on Pet10x get an instant push — 11:00 today just opened, tap to take it — first tap wins, slot filled, nobody phoned anyone. No standalone practice system can do this, because none of them are already in the customer's pocket.

The call list is a first-class surface, not a fallback

A receptionist with twenty minutes between appointments opens one queue, sorted by how overdue each animal is, showing the customer, the pet, what is due, the phone number and the last three contact attempts. Each row offers call — which logs the attempt and opens a note — snooze for a week, mark booked, or suppress. Working a call list is a real job in a practice, and most software treats it as an afterthought behind a bulk-email screen.

Rules the engine holds

  • Suppression is aggressive by default: deceased patients, animals already booked for that reason, customers who unsubscribed, and anyone contacted in the last N days. The existing twenty-four-hour throttle on fine reminders is the pattern to copy.
  • Service reminders and marketing are separate consents and separate tables. A vaccination-due notice is not a newsletter, and merging them eventually costs a practice both.
  • Reminders can come from either space. A vaccination the clinic administered generates one; so can a vaccination expiry the owner recorded and shared. The second is a reason for practices to want their customers linked.
  • Measured in visits booked. The report answers "did this bring animals through the door", which is the only number a practice owner cares about.

08 · Shop, storefront and money

"Shop" splits into three things sharing a spine, and only the first is needed early.

Storefront — the public face

Practice profile, each location with its own hours and phone, services and prices, the team, photos, booking rules, after-hours arrangements. Extends the existing marketplace storefront rather than building a second one, so a clinic appears alongside groomers and walkers with a veterinary treatment.

Retail catalogue

Food, preventatives, supplements, accessories. Sold at the counter, added to a visit, or reserved in the app for pickup. Stock kept simple at first — a count and a reorder point, not lot tracking.

Money

Price list, an estimate the owner can approve in their app before treatment, an invoice built from what was actually done, payment recorded, day sheet, unpaid-balance list. Insurance-claim export is a later, well-defined addition.

One fix has to land before invoicing. Booking money is client-controlled today — the update policies carry no column list, so a customer can set their own booking to paid and rewrite the commission figure. A clinic invoicing real treatment cannot inherit that flaw, so invoicing goes through database functions with a transition table from the first commit, and the existing booking hole gets closed in the same release.

Lot-level inventory with expiry tracking belongs to the EMR track, because its real purpose is tracing a drug recall to the animals that received it — a clinical-safety function rather than a retail one.

09 · Sharing and service records

The two bridges between the spaces, both narrow, both consented, both auditable. This is where a Pet10x customer becomes worth more to the practice than a walk-in.

Owner to clinic — the standing grant

Four ways a grant starts, and no fifth

  • Booking — the confirmation screen offers to share the default three scopes with that clinic until thirty days after the visit. This will be the overwhelming majority.
  • Desk code — owner reads a six-character code to reception, single use, expires in ten minutes. Covers walk-ins, and reuses the existing emergency-token mechanics.
  • Clinic request — arrives as a notification and waits in the owner's app until answered.
  • Owner-initiated — searching for a practice and sharing before a first visit, or when moving cities.

Where the rule lives

In the database, as everything else in this codebase does. A security-definer function resolves an active, unexpired, unrevoked grant for the caller's clinic and the requested scope; row-level policies on every owner-space table consult it; there is no path that reads shared data without passing through it. It is the existing has_booking_for_pet predicate made scope- and time-aware. Every read writes an access-log row — which staff member, which patient, which scope, when.

The sentence the interface must say, in these words: "Revoking stops Harbour Animal Hospital seeing anything new. It does not remove the appointment history and notes they keep in their own system." Vagueness here is how consent features lose trust, and this codebase already has the instinct — one commit is titled "Keep the record, and let the letter go."

Clinic to owner — handing a record back

The reverse bridge, and the one that creates the network effect. At checkout the clinic can publish selected service records into the owner's app: vaccination given with product and next-due date, a vaccination certificate, the visit summary, aftercare instructions, the invoice, the next appointment.

Publication is always an explicit act, never a default. Internal notes, anything about the person rather than the animal, and anything a practice keeps for its own protection stay where they are.

Why this is the strategic centre. Today pet_vaccinations is a text box the owner types into, and a strata manager simply believes it. One published record turns a claim into something with a practice's name attached — which is what the building manager, the boarding kennel, the insurer and the municipal licence application all actually want. Clinic → owner → building is a loop no standalone practice-management product can close, because none of them are in the owner's phone or the building's compliance file.

It also implies a product decision that is yours to make: once a record can be clinic-confirmed, does a self-typed one still satisfy a building requirement? See §18.

10 · The owner's side

Five additions to the owner app. Each is independently useful, and together they are the reason an owner prefers a Pet10x practice.

My vets

Which practices are linked, exactly what each can see, when it expires, one-tap revoke, and the desk code. An owner should be able to answer "who can see my dog's records?" in three seconds.

Records timeline

One history per pet merging what the owner recorded with what practices handed back, each showing where it came from. Exportable as a PDF for a kennel, a new vet, an insurer or a strata.

Book and manage appointments

Book against real availability, get the reminder, see it in the app, reschedule, join a waitlist for a sooner slot.

Reminders that do something

"Rex is due his booster" with a book-now button, instead of a postcard. The reminder and the booking are the same interaction.

Estimates and receipts

Approve an estimate before treatment starts; keep every receipt in one place instead of a glovebox.

Consent asked in context

At booking, at the desk, on referral — never as a settings screen nobody opens.

11 · Emergency

Lighter than revision 1, because the clinic writes no clinical record. What remains is still the most differentiated thing in the plan.

Emergency access — an animal arrives, the owner cannot be reached

A Tier 2 verified practice can pull a narrow emergency projection of the owner's own records without a prior grant, after stating a reason. This is the same category of data the existing building emergency directory already exposes to a first responder, so it is consistent with a precedent this platform has already set.

Returns

  • Species, breed, sex, age, weight, colour
  • Allergies, conditions and medications the owner recorded
  • Owner name and phone number
  • The pet's usual practice, if linked

Never returns

  • Any other practice's records, notes or invoices
  • Documents or photographs
  • Home address or building
  • Anything at all if the practice is not Tier 2

Reason required and retained, owner notified within seconds, every pull logged and rate-limited, and an administrator review queue where abuse costs verification.

The AI handoff — closing a loop that is already half-built

The assistant already runs deterministic red-flag matching before any model replies, and shows an emergency card. Today that card lists the owner's vet from a free-text field, and the nearby-practice rows beneath it are inert — they cannot even be tapped to call, which the interface audit flagged as the worst accessibility failure in the app precisely because it appears on the one screen where seconds matter.

Separately, the existing building emergency directory has a defect worth correcting in this work: it returns behavioural notes and conditions while its own header claims medical history is withheld. Either narrow the projection or change the promise — but decide it deliberately.

15 · The road to full EMR

Full EMR stays the destination. The point of this section is that nothing in releases one to seven has to be unpicked to get there — the operations tables are the EMR's foundation, not a detour around it.

What the EMR track adds, and what it will require

  • Structured clinical notes with signing authority, immutability after finalisation, and amendment trails.
  • Prescriptions with prescriber authority, refills, and dispensing.
  • A controlled-drug register — append-only, isolated in its own module so it can be reviewed or replaced without touching anything else.
  • Lab orders and results with reference ranges; imaging attachments.
  • Formal retention and access-log policies.

The one thing to keep in view. Crossing into the EMR is the point where Pet10x becomes custodian of a regulated medical record, and where the professional advice you have deferred becomes necessary rather than optional. That is a decision for a later quarter with a working product and real practices to inform it — but the crossing is a real one, and the plan should not pretend it is just the next sprint. Everything before it is ordinary business software.

18 · Approve to proceed

No code is written and none will be until you say so. Your reframe answered most of what I had open; four things remain.

1 · Is the operations-versus-clinical line in §01 the right line?

Release one records what was done, what was given and what it cost, but not structured clinical notes, prescriptions or drug custody. If you want vaccination administration recorded more formally than a service record from day one, tell me now — it is a small change early and a large one later.

2 · Do you accept the three-tier model?

Instant signup for everything a practice does with their own data; verification only to receive shared records, publish, or use emergency access. This is what makes self-serve safe. If you want sharing available at Tier 0, say so and I will design compensating controls instead.

3 · Do clinic-confirmed records change strata compliance?

Once a vaccination can carry a practice's name, does a self-typed one still satisfy a building requirement? This affects existing managers and is the highest-value decision in the document — it is what turns the vet module from a second product into a reason the first one wins.

4 · Release order — is R4 in the right place?

I have put the Pet10x advantage fourth, after the standalone product is complete, because a practice must want the software before the network matters. The argument for moving it earlier is that it is the only part competitors cannot copy. I recommend leaving it at four; it is a reasonable thing to disagree with.

On approval I will write this into docs/superpowers/specs/, then produce a task-level implementation plan for R1 and start there. Nothing gets built before that.
